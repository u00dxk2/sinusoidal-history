# Sinusoidal History fact-check: full verification pass

**Bottom line up front:** Six items would survive a careful reader; thirteen would not. The two most embarrassing risks are **B3 (WID 1820 start year + CC BY license — both wrong)** and **A7 (Strauss-Howe Fourth Turning anchored as a "peak" when it is a crisis trough — sign error in the model)**. Either of these is catchable in under a minute by a domain reader and would be a high-conspicuity correction once shared. Section B's data citations contain three license errors and one stale version number; Section A contains one direct contradiction of a theorist's own published statement (Dalio's 1945 peak). Section C's prose contains one logically impossible sentence (C3 — Ibn Khaldun "anchoring" a 1789 event despite dying in 1406) and three psychologizing motivation clauses. Detailed item-by-item verdicts below; final risk ranking at the end.

## Section A — Cycle theories

### A1. Ibn Khaldun

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Period 120 years | **correct** |
| *Muqaddimah* (1377) | **correct** |
| Five-stage cycle (founding → prosperity → luxury → decline → collapse) | **correct-with-caveat** |
| Anchor at 1789 (French Revolution) | **incorrect / editorial overreach** |

**Citations.** Ibn Khaldun, *The Muqaddimah*, trans. Franz Rosenthal (Pantheon/Bollingen, 1958), vol. I, pp. 353–355; Fromherz, *Ibn Khaldun: Life and Times* (Edinburgh, 2010); Dale, *The Orange Trees of Marrakesh* (Harvard, 2015).

**Notes.** 120 years is the dominant scholarly figure (3 generations × 40). The five stages are genuinely Khaldunian — but Rosenthal's labels are (1) consolidation, (2) tyranny, (3) leisure, (4) contentment, (5) waste/disintegration. The site's "founding → prosperity → luxury → decline → collapse" is a moralizing paraphrase that **collapses Khaldun's stages 3 and 4 into "luxury"** and adds frames he does not use as stage names. No Khaldunian reception literature anchors the cycle to 1789; this is project-original.

**Proposed replacement.** Replace stage labels with Rosenthal's, or label them "[paraphrase]." Add to anchor description: *"1789 anchor is this project's editorial choice — Khaldun (d. 1406) made no claim about European history."*

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Yes — anchor language implies Khaldun extended his framework to European events.

### A2. Nikolai Kondratiev

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Period 54 years | **correct-with-caveat** |
| Source: *The Major Economic Cycles* (1925) | **correct-with-caveat** (verging on imprecise) |
| Reference peak: 1973 | **correct-with-caveat** |
| Confidence "empirical-contested" | **correct** |

**Citations.** Kondratiev, "Большие циклы конъюнктуры," *Voprosy Konyunktury* 1 (1925); "Die langen Wellen der Konjunktur," *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft* 56 (1926); "The Long Waves in Economic Life," *Review of Economic Statistics* 17(6) (1935); Freeman & Louçã, *As Time Goes By* (Oxford, 2001); Korotayev & Tsirel (2010).

**Notes.** "*The Major Economic Cycles* (1925)" is a real work but it is the **Russian-language original**; an English-reader who looks up "Major Economic Cycles 1925" will not find an English book by that title. The accessible English-reader citation is the 1935 abridgment in *Review of Economic Statistics*. 54y is inside the conventional 45–60 range. 1973 is a defensible anchor but not unanimous — Mandel preferred 1968, others 1971/1974; "1968–1974 transition window" is the safer scholarly framing.

**Proposed replacement.** *"N.D. Kondratiev, 'The Major Economic Cycles' / 'Bol'shie tsikly kon'yunktury,' Voprosy Konyunktury 1 (1925); abridged English: 'The Long Waves in Economic Life,' Review of Economic Statistics 17(6) (1935). 4th-wave peak conventionally placed in the 1968–1974 window; 1973 chosen here as anchor."*

### A3. Samuel Huntington

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1981 / Belknap-Harvard | **correct** |
| Period 60 years | **imprecise** |
| Reference peak 1965 | **imprecise / editorial overreach** |

**Citations.** Huntington, *American Politics: The Promise of Disharmony* (Belknap/Harvard, 1981), Ch. 5 and Ch. 7 ("The S&S Years, 1960–1975"); Brogan review, *New Republic* (1982); Harvard Crimson review, "The Uses of Passion" (24 Feb 1982).

**Notes.** Huntington's literal section title is **"The S&S Years, 1960–1975"** — a 15-year *interval*, not a single peak year. Secondary literature consistently glosses the period as "**60 to 70 years**" between peaks, not exactly 60. The four-period pattern (Revolutionary 1770s → Jacksonian 1820s–30s → Progressive 1890s–1900s → S&S 1960s–70s) yields gaps averaging ~63 years. **No mainstream secondary source pinpoints 1965 as Huntington's peak.** Mid-window (~1968) better reflects his text; 1965 privileges civil-rights specifically over Vietnam/anti-establishment, which Huntington weights together.

**Proposed replacement.** Period: *"~60–70 years."* Anchor gloss: *"Huntington's 'S&S Years' span 1960–1975; we anchor at the interval midpoint (~1968). Huntington does not designate a single peak year."*

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Yes on the precise 1965 anchor; the prose claim (C1) compounds it.

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Period 55 years | **correct-with-caveat** |
| Source: *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (2002) | **correct** |
| Reference peak: 2000 (end of installation) | **imprecise / editorial overreach** |
| Confidence tier "quantitative" | **incorrect / significant editorial overreach** |

**Citations.** Perez, *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (Edward Elgar, 2002), Ch. 3–5; Perez, "The double bubble at the turn of the century," *Cambridge Journal of Economics* 33(4) (2009), pp. 779–805.

**Notes.** Perez calls 2000–2001 the **"Turning Point"** between Installation and Deployment — the frenzy/bubble apex *and crash*, not a "peak" of the surge. The surge's true maturity peak is decades later (mid-Deployment). She has stated as recently as 2019 that the ICT surge has not yet reached its Golden Age peak. More importantly, **"quantitative" misrepresents her methodology**. Perez does qualitative historical pattern recognition (Schumpeterian periodization) — no statistical models, no time-series fitting, no formal forecasts. Pairing her with Turchin (formal structural-demographic models, preregistered 2010 *Nature* forecast) in a "quantitative" tier overstates her methodological mode by a full tier.

**Proposed replacement.** Confidence tier: **change "quantitative" → "empirical-contested"** (same tier as Kondratiev). Anchor gloss: *"Dot-com bubble (2000) — Perez's 'Turning Point' between Installation and Deployment phases of the ICT surge, not a 'peak' in her terminology."*

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Yes — both the "peak" framing and the tier classification.

### A6. Ray Dalio

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Period 85 years | **correct-with-caveat** |
| Source: *Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order* (2021) | **correct** |
| Reference peak 1945 | **imprecise / direct contradiction of Dalio's own statement** |

**Citations.** Dalio, *Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order* (Avid Reader Press, 2021), esp. Ch. 2, Ch. 5 ("The Big Cycles of the United States and the Dollar, Part 1"); Bridgewater research and Asia Business Council PDF (Sept 2020).

**Notes.** Dalio's own published wording in Ch. 5: *"these measures of the United States' powers relative to its own history reached their peaks **in the 1950s** immediately after the Allies won World War II."* The Bridgewater empire-score chart visually peaks ~1950, not 1945. Independent reuses (Mauldin Economics, Allio Capital, Chandler Nguyen) all read the chart as peaking ~1950. **"1945" conflates the founding of the new world order with the empire-score peak — a substantive error on Dalio's own terms.** On period: 75 is Dalio's most-cited central value ("about 75 years on average"); 85 is at the upper edge of his "50–100 / 75±30" range.

**Proposed replacement.** *"Reference peak: ~1950 — Dalio explicitly states the US power index reached its maximum in the 1950s (Changing World Order, Ch. 5). 1945 marked the founding of the new world order; the composite peak is later."* Period: consider 75 over 85.

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Yes — high. Directly contradicts Dalio's published statement.

### A7. Strauss & Howe

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Period 84 years | **correct** |
| Source: *The Fourth Turning* (1997) | **correct** |
| Imprint: Broadway Books | **correct** |
| Reference peak 2008 | **incorrect — sign error in the model** |

**Citations.** Strauss & Howe, *The Fourth Turning: An American Prophecy* (Broadway Books, 1997), Chs. 1–2; Wikipedia "Strauss–Howe generational theory"; CDAMM bibliographic entry.

**Notes — critical.** **The Fourth Turning is a crisis nadir, not a peak.** It is winter in the saecular cycle: a "decisive era of secular upheaval" comparable to the Civil War or Great Depression+WWII. The First Turning (High) is the saeculum's peak; the Second (Awakening) is a cultural/spiritual peak; the Fourth is the trough. Strauss & Howe's own 1997 forecast: *"If the Crisis catalyst comes on schedule, around the year 2005, then the climax will be due around 2020, the resolution around 2026."* So 2008 is the **catalyst/onset**, not the climax — and even the climax is a trough, not a peak. Anchoring a sinusoid's *peak* at 2008 with an 84-year period implies prior peaks at 1924 and prior trough at 1966 — none of which align with Strauss-Howe's actual chronology (post-WWII High peak ~1955, Consciousness Revolution awakening peak mid-1960s–early 1980s, Civil War Crisis 1860s, Depression+WWII Crisis 1929–45).

**Proposed replacement.** Either:
- **(a) Re-anchor as a TROUGH at ~2020** (Strauss-Howe's published Crisis-climax forecast), or
- **(b) Re-anchor as a PEAK at ~1955** (post-WWII American High peak, the most recent saecular high).

The 2008-as-peak anchor is mathematically self-contradictory with Strauss-Howe's own framework and should be flagged as the project's interpretive overlay rather than presented as Strauss-Howe's identification of a peak.

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Critical.

### B2. Fernald TFP (FRBSF)

**Verdict: correct-with-caveat.**

URL is live (last updated 3/31/2026). Methodology citation on page: Fernald (2014), FRBSF WP 2012-19. The underlying quarterly series begins **1947Q2**, not 1948. Fernald is now Economist Emeritus at FRBSF; operational maintenance has shifted to FRBSF staff (Shane Boyle).

**Critical:** **5-year centered rolling average is the project's transformation, not a Fernald-published presentation.** A centered 5-year window applied to a series starting 1947Q2 cannot produce a value before ~1949Q4 (~1950), so a displayed start of "1948" is internally inconsistent with the stated transform. The right endpoint is also chopped by ~2 years.

**Proposed replacement.** *"Underlying series: FRBSF Quarterly Utilization-Adjusted TFP (Fernald 2014, WP 2012-19), 1947Q2–2025Q4. Displayed: 5-year centered rolling mean computed by [project], ~1950–2023."* License: *"freely downloadable; © FRBSF; no explicit reuse license."*

**Editorial-overreach flag.** Medium — transformation should be attributed to project, not Fernald.

### B3. WID Top 1% Wealth Share — **CRITICAL**

**Verdict: incorrect** (license and coverage start year are both wrong).

URL https://wid.world/country/usa/ is live. Two material errors:

**1. License.** Page's license badge links to **https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/** — i.e., **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, not CC BY. The World Inequality Report 2022 confirms the same: "Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International License – CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, World Inequality Lab." This is a substantive rights difference: commercial reuse is prohibited; ShareAlike is required. Anyone redistributing WID data under "CC BY" terms could be infringing.

**2. Coverage.** WID's flagship US top-1% wealth share series rests on **Saez & Zucman (2016), "Wealth Inequality in the United States since 1913: Evidence from Capitalized Income Tax Data," QJE 131(2):519–578**. The title itself says it: **since 1913**. The paper explicitly notes that "for the 1913–1945 period, we combine balance sheets from Goldsmith et al. (1956), Wolff (1989), and Kopczuk and Saez (2004)." There is no Saez-Zucman wealth-share data point before 1913.

The "1820" start may be a confusion with the World Inequality Report 2022 *global* income-inequality reconstruction (which does extend to 1820), but that is a global income series, not a US wealth-shares series. **A "1820–2024" coverage claim for the US top-1% wealth share is materially misleading.**

**Proposed replacement.** Source: *"WID · World Inequality Database (Saez–Zucman 2016 / DINA)."* License: **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (not CC BY). Coverage: *"1913–most-recent (Saez–Zucman capitalized-income series for US top-1% adult wealth share; pre-1913 wealth concentration is not published in this WID series)."* If pre-1913 splicing is in the local CSV, cite the third-party source separately (Lindert; Williamson; Sutch) and disclose the splice.

**Editorial-overreach flag.** High / critical.

### B4. Maddison Project Database 2023

**Verdict: correct-with-caveat.**

URL https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023 is live; license **CC BY 4.0** confirmed verbatim on page. Citation: Bolt & van Zanden, *Journal of Economic Surveys* (2024/25), DOI 10.1111/joes.12618.

**Coverage "1870–2022" is imprecise.** MPD 2023 covers 169 countries up to 2022 and includes country series back to 1 CE; regional aggregates start ~1820. Continuous annual coverage of major economies generally does start ~1870, so the trim is defensible *as an editorial choice* — but the dataset is not bounded at 1870.

**Critical: "1945 (~42%) is exactly Dalio's reference peak year" is unverifiable from public sources at this fidelity, and the framing is editorial overreach.** MPD does not pre-publish a "US share of world GDP" series; it must be computed from per-capita GDP × population in 2011 PPP \$. Published Maddison-derived US-share figures cluster 27–40% for the WWII era at PPP, with the peak commonly placed in **1944** (peak war production), not 1945. The 50%-ish figures sometimes cited use nominal USD, not Maddison PPP. Tethering MPD to "Dalio's reference peak" conflates a research dataset with an external commentator's narrative; MPD makes no claim about a US peak share.

**Proposed replacement.** Coverage: *"1820–2022 (regional aggregates); some country series to 1 CE; trimmed to 1870 for continuous coverage of major economies."* `association_note`: *"US share computed from MPD 2023 GDP per capita × population (2011 PPP \$). WWII-era peak appears around 1944–45 at roughly 35–40%, sensitive to PPP basis. Year-of-peak coincides loosely with the 1945 anchor used here for Dalio."*

## Section C — Prose claims

### C1. "Huntington anchored on 1965 because he was writing in 1981 and the civil-rights surge dominated his view."
**Verdict: imprecise + editorial overreach.** Huntington dated the period as 1960–1975 ("S&S Years"), not 1965; the motivation is psychologizing.
**Proposed replacement.** *"Huntington dated his fourth creedal-passion period as 1960–1975 ('the S&S years'); we anchor at the interval midpoint. The choice is ours, not his."*

### C2. "Kondratiev-wave popularisers anchored on 1973 because the oil shock was vivid to them."
**Verdict: imprecise + editorial overreach.** Mandel preferred 1968; Mensch focused on innovation troughs in the 1930s; Wallerstein used a 1967–73 phase-shift; Korotayev locates the K4/K5 transition as "1973–74" but with a 1968–74 window. "Because the oil shock was vivid to them" is unsourced psychologizing.
**Proposed replacement.** *"K-wave revivalists in the late 1970s and early 1980s variously dated the 4th-wave turning point between 1968 and 1974, often clustering around the 1973–74 stagflation episode. We use 1973 as a conventional anchor."*

### C3. "Khaldun anchored a European Enlightenment collapse because his later Western readers needed the framework legible in their own history." — **CRITICAL**
**Verdict: incorrect (logically impossible) + severe editorial overreach.** Ibn Khaldun died in 1406; he could not have anchored a 1789 event. The sentence reads as if Khaldun himself made this choice.
**Proposed replacement.** *"We anchor our Khaldunian cycle at 1789 (French Revolution) so the framework — originally developed by Ibn Khaldun (d. 1406) for North African dynasties — can be tested against the modern European historical record our other data series cover. This is our editorial choice; Khaldun himself made no claim about European history."*

### C4. "Perez anchors on 2000 because the dot-com bubble was the defining technology-finance event of her career."
**Verdict: imprecise + editorial overreach.** Perez identifies 2000–2001 as a **Turning Point** between phases, not a sinusoid peak; she has explicitly extended the turning-point window to 2008. Motivation attribution is psychologizing.
**Proposed replacement.** *"Perez identifies the dot-com crash (2000–2001) as the Turning Point between Installation and Deployment phases of the 5th surge. We anchor our sinusoid's peak there; this conflates Perez's Turning-Point concept with our peak representation."*

### C5. "Kondratiev waves have never been cleanly confirmed in empirical long-run data."
**Verdict: correct-with-caveat.** Defensible. Spectral analyses (Korotayev–Tsirel 2010, Metz 1992) report some periodicity at weak significance; Metz (2011) and Diebolt & Doliger are skeptical; mainstream macro rejects the theory. "Never cleanly confirmed" is the right framing.
**Optional softening.** *"…remain contested in empirical long-run data: some spectral and unobserved-components analyses report a detectable ~50-year periodicity, but period, phase dating, and existence are not agreed across studies, and the theory is rejected by most mainstream macroeconomists."*

### C6. "Strauss-Howe is generational theory — influential in popular discourse, contested in academic history."
**Verdict: correct.** Influence (Gore distributing *Generations*; LifeCourse Associates; corporate marketing penetration) and contestation (Greenberg in *Politico* April 2017 calling it "crackpot"; Lind's review; Turchin's published critique that it "forces the historical record to fit") are both well-documented. No change.
