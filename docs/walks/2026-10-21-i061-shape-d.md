## Shape (d), stated accurately

Each cycle is drawn as **one period centred on the reference peak this site stores for it** (`reference_peak_year` in `src/data/cycles.json`), and nothing else. **That anchor is the site's, not always the writer's.** For Turchin's 150-year cycle it sits in his published 2020s window. Each cycle's page states the basis for its anchor, and for Khaldun (1789), Huntington, Schlesinger Jr., Perez and Modelski the cycles page itself says it is the site's construction, and for Dalio it is the site's join of two of his constructs (Codex r4 finding 2; `cycles.json:8, 33, 45, 58, 83, 110`).

What changes for a reader: the site stops drawing a repeating wave and stops stating where the present sits on one. The wave is the site's arithmetic. Every reader confusion on record (I-047, I-048, I-054, I-055, I-059, I-060, I-061) came from that repeating wave or from a sentence reading a position off it.

**Rule (d'), proposed:** draw `[peak − P/2, min(peak + P/2, current year)]`. No year after the present is drawn.

**What (d') does and does not fix, honestly:**
- **Dalio** draws 1912.5–1987.5, crest 1950. There is no 2025 crest anywhere. This is the case the row exists for, and (d') removes it.
- **Perez** stops 1.5 years before its trough, at cos ≈ −0.99. Nearly a whole period.
- **Turchin (150-year)** draws trough 1945 → crest 2020 → six years falling, ending at cos ≈ +0.97. **That is the same height the rejected shape (c) left Dalio at (2022, +0.97), on the opposite slope.** (d') differs from (c) in that it removes every repeated period and cuts at today, not at the record's end. But for Turchin, a reader still sees a crest at the right edge, near now. The 2020s crest matches Turchin's published forecast window (Ages of Discord, End Times; `cycles.json:70`). The exact waveform (a symmetric cosine with a 1945 trough) is the site's. Whether readers take this stub as the site agreeing is exactly what the walk has to find out. **Not fixed by construction; scored by the walk.**
- The brush still sets each card's axis, so on a range that does not contain a cycle's window that card draws a fragment or nothing (for example, Khaldun on Modern 1900–2050). Recorded; the walk asks about it.
