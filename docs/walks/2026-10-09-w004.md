# W-004 cold walk, 2026-10-09

**N=1, synthetic.** One agent walked the live site with a scripted headless browser. It is not a user, and nothing here is a user's reaction. Treat each finding as a hypothesis about what a stranger might see.

- **Browser:** Playwright Chromium, headless, from `node_modules` in this repo.
- **Viewports:** phone 390x664 (`devices["iPhone 15"]`, isMobile, hasTouch); desktop 900x900 and 1440x900, mouse.
- **Site:** https://sinusoidalhistory.com, live, read 2026-10-09.
- **What was not read:** no repo source, docs, continuity, `public/` or `.md` files; no `/llms.txt` or `.md` URL. From the 10-07 walk folder only the `.mjs` driver scripts were read, for launch and dump shape. Its dumps and notes were not opened.
- **Drivers and dumps:** `tmp/walk-2026-10-09/` (`dump.mjs`, `s1.mjs`, `s1b.mjs`, `s2.mjs`, `s2b.mjs`, `s3.mjs`, plus `*-blocks.txt`, `*-visible.txt`, `*.png`).

## Instrument notes

- Touch gestures were not used to scroll. Page scroll used `scrollTo`, and the figure was moved sideways by setting `scrollLeft` on its scroll region. A real swipe was not tested.
- Headless Chromium draws no scrollbar track here (track height measured 0 px). So I cannot say whether a desktop user on Windows would see a horizontal scrollbar under the figure. A headed browser would answer that.
- `y` values are page y in CSS px, read from `getBoundingClientRect().top + scrollY`. Screen counts are y divided by the viewport height.
- Full-page screenshots resize the page for a moment. The block dumps taken right after one showed the desktop cue text on the phone. `s1b.mjs` re-read the phone cue four ways (at load, after 3 s, in view, back at top) and every read was the phone cue. The phone answers below use those reads, not the dump.
- The figure image is in the page twice. Only the visible copy was measured.

## Step 2. Desktop 900x900, mouse, /cycles/kondratiev, same figure

**Did.** Loaded the page, scrolled the figure to 200 px from the top, read the screen. Measured the scroll region. Hovered the figure and read the cursor. Tried a plain wheel, a Shift+wheel and a horizontal wheel over it. In a separate run, clicked the figure once.

**Saw.** The figure is 900 px wide inside a 704 px window onto it (page x 98 to 802). The right 196 px is clipped. The legend at top right is cut at "series (solid) · 54y", and the "2025" end label is not shown. There is no fade or arrow at the edge. The cursor over the figure is a pointer. The cue above it, at y 2622, read "Scroll sideways for the whole figure, or click it to open it full size".

Plain wheel down over the figure: the page scrolled 200 px, the figure did not move (scrollLeft 0). Shift+wheel: the figure moved 196 px, its full extent. Horizontal wheel: also works.

One click: the same tab went to `https://sinusoidalhistory.com/data/spectral/kondratiev.svg`. No new tab, no dialog. The bare figure filled the window, whole, with no site header or links. Getting back to the page needs the browser's Back.

**Answer.**
(a) The cue told me to scroll sideways or click for full size. The click half fits a mouse user well. The scroll half is half true for a mouse: a plain wheel scrolls the page, not the figure. It takes Shift+wheel or a trackpad. The cue does not say so.
> "Scroll sideways for the whole figure, or click it to open it full size" (y 2622)

(b) No. About a fifth of the figure was off to the right. I could tell only because the legend was cut mid-phrase. I knew how to get the rest from the cue's "click it" part. I did not know from the cue how to scroll it with a mouse wheel.

(c) The page was replaced by the raw SVG file at full size. It showed the whole figure. It left the site.

(d) No, nothing reads as a test having been run. The caption opens by saying none was.
> "No test was run: this record covers 1.4 of the 3.0 full periods the pre-registered test requires, so the spectrum in the figure is descriptive only." (y 3238)

What pulls the other way, mildly: the figure footer says "spectral verdict", the spectrum carries a "target: 54y" marker, and the caption's second sentence is technical and specific.
> "Had the record qualified, the test would be a harmonic regression at the exact stated period against an AR(1) red-noise null (99,999 bootstrap draws)." (y 3238)

A skimmer who reads "99,999 bootstrap draws" without "Had the record qualified" could think draws were made. What pulls against it, on the figure itself: "descriptive only" under the band, and the band "INSUFFICIENT SPAN — 1.4 OF 3.0 REQUIRED PERIODS".

**Frank note.** At 900 px the figure is wider than its window and nothing visual says so. The cue text is the only signal. The click is honest ("open it full size") but drops the reader out of the site with no way back except Back.

**Answer.**

> "The record this verdict is judged on, US top 1% wealth share (1913 onward), runs 111 years: 0.7 of the 3.0 full periods this site requires before it will run a test on a 150-year claim." (y 551)

(b) The "Paired data" line names the whole wealth-share series. The chart draws it from 1820, but before 1913 there are only five rough points joined into one line. The verdict counts only the yearly record from 1913. So the paired series is longer on the chart than the record that was measured.
> "Why two labels: the “Paired data” line and section on this page, and the citation at the end, name the whole US Top 1% Wealth Share series, which the interactive chart draws from 1820. The verdict counts only the yearly record, which starts in 1913 with the Saez–Zucman series." (y 2512)

The question arises at the Paired data line (y 919). The reason is 1,593 px below it, about 1.8 screens. From the verdict sentence (y 551) it is 1,961 px. Nothing at y 919 points down to it.

Honestly, as a cold reader I would probably not have asked the question. "TOP 1% WEALTH SHARE" looks like a short form of "US top 1% wealth share (1913 onward)". The difference is only the "(1913 onward)", which is easy to read past.

(c) No. Every place that could suggest a test says one was not run.

Mild pulls: the "Spectral verdict" heading, and the classification line, which is about Turchin's own statistics, not the site's.
> "Confidence classification: Quantitative — the period comes from statistical work on historical data." (y 1507)

**Frank note.** (a) and (c) are clean. (b) works only if you scroll to the verdict section. The two labels are close enough that most readers will not notice them differ, which is both why the gap is harmless and why the explanation may never be read.

## Step 5. Desktop 1440x900, /methods, cold

**Did.** Loaded the page and read it top to bottom from the block dump and the first screen.

**Saw.** The intro says "0 of the 9 pairings" are long enough to test (y 422). Long data-sources list, then Normalization, then "Why Pearson is the wrong tool" (y 3964), then "Spectral testing" (y 4838): a table where every row reads "Insufficient data — no test possible", then three long paragraphs about the protocol.

**Answer.**
(a) The site draws ten cycle theories as pure sine waves and lays real data over nine of them. It says none of the nine data records is long enough, at three full cycles, to test whether the theory's rhythm is really there, so no test was run and that shortage is itself the result.
> "The result to read first: under a pre-registered spectral protocol, 0 of the 9 pairings have a record long enough (three full periods) to be tested at the theory's own period." (y 422)

(b) The page says clearly, more than once, that no test was run on the nine. Two places still read as though something ran.

First, the paragraph that opens the protocol describes the test in the present tense, with p-values and corrections, before the next paragraph says nothing reached it.
> "Since August 2026 every cycle–series pairing carries a pre-registered spectral verdict, computed by a committed script (scripts/spectral_verdict.py) from a frozen analysis manifest and published at /data/spectral/verdicts.json with one figure per pairing." (y 5601)
> "Multiple tests are Holm-corrected within pre-registered families." (y 5601)

Second, a sentence says some re-pairings do clear the gate. It does not say whether they were tested or what came out, and I found no such panel on the page.
> "A secondary cross-grid panel re-pairs each period with every series long enough to clear the gate (19 cells, labelled as re-pairings, not the site's claims)." (y 5984)

Also the intro link "How the test works →" (y 422) talks of the test as a working thing. On balance the page does not claim a result for the nine, but the cross-grid sentence leaves open that tests ran elsewhere.

(c) The sentence that confused me most:
> "A secondary cross-grid panel re-pairs each period with every series long enough to clear the gate (19 cells, labelled as re-pairings, not the site's claims)." (y 5984)

Where is this panel? Were those 19 cells tested? If so, what did they show? The page does not say.

**Frank note.** The table (y 4886 to 5524) is the clearest thing on the page and it comes first, which helps. The 380 px paragraph after it is written for a reader who already knows no test ran. A stranger who skims from the heading to that paragraph gets "p-value", "bootstrap" and "Holm-corrected" before the "no code path emits a p-value" line.

**Screenshots.** `m-desk-first.png`, `m-desk-full.png`.

## The single worst thing

On /methods, one sentence says that 19 re-pairings clear the 3-period gate, and then says nothing more. A cold reader who has just been told "0 of the 9" now learns that some data is long enough after all, and cannot find out whether those cells were tested or what they showed. It is the one place on the walk where the site implies a test may have run and produced a result without saying so.
> "A secondary cross-grid panel re-pairs each period with every series long enough to clear the gate (19 cells, labelled as re-pairings, not the site's claims)." (/methods, y 5984)
