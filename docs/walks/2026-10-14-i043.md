## Answer to the question
Each row takes the cycle length that one theory claims (Schlesinger Jr.'s 30 years, Turchin's 50, Kondratiev's 54, and so on) and checks whether that rhythm shows up in a different historical data series, one the theory isn't normally matched with, like US polarization or war deaths. The row tells you how long that record is, how many of those cycles fit inside it, and a "p" number. As far as I can tell, the p number says how easily a cycle-less, randomly wandering series could match that rhythm just as well. Small means "hard to get by chance". There is also a second p, from a different version of "random wandering", as a cross-check. To count, a p would have to be below 0.05. **None of the 19 came out significant.** The lowest is 0.094 (Schlesinger's 30 years against the V-Dem democracy index), and its second-opinion number is higher still (0.185).
