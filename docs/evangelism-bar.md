## The ONE core problem

"Someone told me history runs in cycles — Dalio, Strauss–Howe, Turchin — and I can't tell whether any of it holds up or is just a good story."

HYPOTHESIS. It is phrased the way a reader would ask it. The only real-user signal on record is Search Console: the first non-brand queries (2026-09-16) were Ray Dalio searches, ranked 49–60. No reader has written to the site.

## The would-tell-a-friend moment

A reader lands on a cycle they have heard about. On one screen they see its curve drawn against a real measured series, and they get a plain verdict on whether the record can even test it. The verdict comes from a pre-registered test the site would run, never from the site's opinion. Then they send the page to the friend who quoted that cycle at them: "look, someone actually checked."

The effect is honest resolution of an argument. Sharing a /cycles/<slug> URL is only the channel it travels through.

**What breaks the moment:** any sentence that overclaims. If the page says a test ran when none did, it becomes one more confident story. That is why I-024 and I-028 (the "a test ran" sentences on Kondratiev and Turchin) are bar work, not polish.

## The proving metric

**Qualitative real-user evidence** (`real-user-evidence`), the honest instrument at N < 40. The signal is an unprompted share or mention of a /cycles page with a note like "someone checked".

**Instrument not yet built. This bar reads UNMEASURED.** The site carries no client analytics, by standing choice. Search Console (`node scripts/gsc-read.mjs --start <date>`) sees who was *shown* a page and who clicked through. It cannot see a share. The Render request logs (`scripts/crawl-read.mjs`) see crawlers. Product ships proceed and are reported as unmeasured. If anything is instrumented, it is proportionate and sits alongside a ship, never in place of one.
